# M02 — Investment area & money flow (theme discovery)

Status: design complete 2026-10-09 (D-M02-1…16) · Build: parts 1, 2, 4, 4b done (D-M02-17, -18, -22…27); part-3 pre-check done (D-M02-28…31); part 3 built (D-M02-32…34); part 5 built (D-M02-35, -36); parts 5–7 in M02_BUILD_LOG.md · Updated: 2026-10-09 · Outline headings: Select the investment area; Follow the money; Identify capex recipients · Depends on: M00 (data, storage, search/LLM) · Sources: S16, S24, S44

## Purpose
Spot emerging themes in filings and news, follow the money (who spends, who receives), and suggest themes and candidate companies for the owner to accept or reject.

## Decisions carried
- Engine discovers themes in filings and news and makes suggestions; owner decides (D23).
- Reuse owner's retrieval code via M00 (D4).
- LLM only on stored, verified passages (D21).
- Leading filing data: RPO, purchase obligations, ≥10% customers (A1).
- Every fact carries an evidence grade (D14).

## Inputs available (M00 built)
- Phrase counts over time across all filers, as documents, distinct filings and distinct companies per window (`fulltext.py`, D-SEC-14).
- Local corpus of watchlist and peer filings, split into sections (Tier 2, D-SRCH-4).
- XBRL capex, RPO, purchase obligations and peer statistics (`xbrl_facts`, `frames`).
- 8-K events and stored EX-99.1 press releases (`events`, `documents`).
- Customer–supplier links from ≥10% customer disclosures; customers' news leads suppliers' prices (S44) — to build.

## Decisions
- D-M02-1 (2026-10-07): A theme is a named set of phrases (one EDGAR full-text query per phrase, merged by accession) plus linked companies and money-flow links (customer capex → supplier RPO/backlog). Both sources: the owner seeds themes; the engine proposes new phrases whose distinct-filer counts rise; the owner accepts or rejects proposals (D23).
- D-M02-2 (2026-10-07): News in two phases. Phase 1, grade A only: filings, 8-K press releases, company IR RSS for watchlist and peers (owner's `ir_feeds.py` as a start). Phase 2: curated free trade-press RSS per theme, grade B. News is stored as headline, date, link and short snippet only (copyright); the LLM reads only stored passages (D21). Broad news indexes (e.g. GDELT) not planned.
- D-M02-3 (2026-10-07): Suggestions show their components side by side — growth in distinct filers vs the prior window, breadth across companies and industries, money flow (capex, RPO, purchase obligations, ≥10% customers), novelty, evidence grade — with no single combined score until backtested. Weekly scan delivered as a digest in the review queue (D8), plus a deeper pass after each 10-Q/10-K season.
- D-M02-5 (2026-10-08, was P-M02-1): Capex guidance is a money-flow component (grade A, quote link), counted as **positive for the spender and for the theme's suppliers** (owner: capex shows a company able and willing to invest in its future, as a buyback shows management's view of the best use of excess capital; weighting revisited later. Research favoured suppliers only — see failure modes). Code computes per spender: guided growth vs the last reported year on the company's own definition (Meta = PP&E payments + finance-lease principal), revision since the previous guidance, and actual vs guided after year end (R19); labelled "hypothesis, not backtested" until a backtest exists (D-M02-3). Each capex row keeps its "including/excluding …" clause; a change between rows with different clauses is "definition changed", not raised/lowered (owner can override in review). A newer release without a capex figure flags the row "may have been updated on the call (DS1)". A spender counts toward a theme only through a quoted sentence tying its spending to the theme's phrases (code finds, owner confirms). Supplier search favours less-covered second-tier suppliers (lead-lag shrinks with coverage, S74). No cross-company totals in v1; each company keeps its period label. Beside the guidance, the spender shows whether capex is covered by operating cash flow or funded by new debt or shares (XBRL; separates "able and willing" from stretching; owner). Sources S71–S81.
- D-M02-7 (2026-10-08): Supplier discovery is an evidence ladder; each customer–supplier link stores its rung, grade and quote. **v1 builds L0–L3 (owner):** L0 input categories a company depends on (from its own filings or an LLM proposal verified by quote; owner confirms; grade C until a named link exists); L1 customer names the supplier (A); L2 supplier names the customer — reverse full-text lookup of the customer's name near customer/revenue-share wording in other filers' 10-K/10-Q (A; how Cohen–Frazzini's link data was built, S44); L3 contracts naming both parties (8-K Item 1.01, Exhibit 10 supply/capacity agreements) (A). **Later parts:** L4 order-flow matching (customer purchase obligations/prepayments ↔ supplier RPO/backlog, contract liabilities, inventory, capex, a new ≥10% customer; inference, grade C); L5 bottleneck and pricing power ("sold out", "allocation", "lead times" + gross margin vs own history); L6 foreign indicators (Taiwan monthly revenue, Korea chip exports, US Census trade — free access to verify); L7 customer-to-supplier return lead (with M09); L8 Hoberg–Phillips vertical links (annual, Compustat-keyed). Less-covered focus: proxy = market cap and number of 13F holders (analyst coverage is not free). An unnamed customer stays unknown; matching "Customer A" by revenue share is labelled inference.
- D-M02-6 (2026-10-08): Call-only guidance (DS1) comes from **company-published IR documents** (prepared remarks, transcripts, slides) for the main spenders first, fetched through one adapter, stored whole like filings (company disclosures, not news, so D-M02-2's snippet rule does not apply) and parsed by the same guidance parser; third-party transcripts excluded. **Revised same day after checking IR sites (owner):** first source = Alpha Vantage `EARNINGS_CALL_TRANSCRIPT` (free key `ALPHA_VANTAGE_API_KEY`; probe `scripts/probe_alpha_vantage.py` decides coverage); if it fails (or for documents it lacks, e.g. prepared remarks), use company IR documents while **honouring each site's terms (owner)**: the engine fetches automatically only where terms allow (Microsoft, Marvell, GlobalFoundries; Micron at human pace); for sites whose terms bar robots or whose terms are unclear (NVIDIA, Apple, Intel, Boeing, Oracle, Amazon, Alphabet via robots.txt, Meta, Tesla) the owner downloads the file in the browser into an **inbox folder** and the engine stores and parses it. After a company's earnings 8-K arrives, the engine lists the documents to download with their IR page links (review item `m02_ir_download`), so the workaround takes minutes per season. The logon catch-up ends with a Windows summary box when there is something to download or review (scheduler script only, U6). An **"I downloaded it"** button finds the newest matching PDF/DOCX/PPTX in the owner's Downloads folder (configurable), shows the file name and ingests on confirm — no moving files (owner). Third-party transcripts are grade **B**, upgraded to A when the same figure appears in a company document or later filing. Only 5 of 13 checked companies post full transcripts (Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, NVIDIA and Boeing — the last two FactSet ©); Micron and Intel post prepared remarks with capex; Amazon publishes call audio only → local transcription logged as a future task (DS9). **Probe 2026-10-09 (owner ran `probe_alpha_vantage.py`):** free key works (7/7 HTTP 200, no limit messages); covers AMZN, MSFT, ORCL, AAPL, TSLA (35–57k characters per call, speaker turns with name and title); `quarter` is the **company's fiscal label** (MSFT 2026Q2 = FY26 Q2 call reporting $37.5B capex; 2026Q4 = July 2026 call; ORCL 2026Q3 = March 2026 call); ORCL 2027Q1 (Sept 2026 call) came back empty → empty = not available yet, retried, then the IR fallback. Amazon is covered, so DS9 audio transcription drops to companies Alpha Vantage misses. Reason: in 2026 only Meta's capex guidance is in its release; Alphabet ($195–205B), Amazon (~$220B), Microsoft (~$175B CY2026) and Oracle (FY27 $90–95B) gave current figures on calls, so the ledger's earlier values are stale (S80) (owner).
- D-M02-8 (2026-10-08, was P-M02-2, phrases): **Code proposes phrases; Gemini names them.** Each season code counts 2–3-word phrases across the stored corpus by year and flags rising ones (Hanley–Hoberg style, S83); EFTS then checks whether each is spreading across all filers (D-M02-1). Gemini only names a cluster and suggests variants (use case 4), which pass the same tests. A phrase reaches the owner only with: EFTS hits; ≥ N distinct companies (N set at build, e.g. 5); not spread across unrelated industries (too generic); 3 sample passages from the corpus; zero hits shown as zero. Reason: LLM query expansion fails where the model lacks topic knowledge — i.e. new themes (S82); generic mentions inflate counts (S84) (owner).
- D-M02-9 (2026-10-08, was P-M02-2, links): **Code finds, Gemini labels, owner confirms.** Code selects candidate passages (L1–L3, D-M02-7); Gemini labels direction and type (supplier / customer / partner / competitor) with a quote naming both companies (use case 5); every link is a review item. Before link counts feed anything, precision and direction accuracy are measured on ~30 hand-labelled corpus passages. Missing link = unknown. Reason: LLM extraction is precise but low-recall, and direction is the main error (S85). New use case **5b approved**: input categories a company depends on (rung L0), each quoted from its own Item 1/1A; owner-initiated, one call per company, cost shown first (D-SRCH-21) (owner).
- D-M02-10 (2026-10-08, was P-M02-3): **Digest.** Two cadences (refines D-M02-3): a **weekly event digest** (new or revised capex guidance, link candidates, 8-K/IR news on accepted themes, a company mentioning an accepted theme for the first time) and a **seasonal deep pass** (phrase mining, EFTS breadth, theme and candidate proposals) ~mid-April and ~50 days after each quarter end; both code-only and may be scheduled; Gemini only on owner request (D-SRCH-18). Breadth = theme's share of distinct filers of the same form family, compared with the same window a year earlier (S88). Delivery: one review item per proposal, grouped under a weekly header, **cap 10 new items/week**, overflow shown in the Themes tab only; repeats suppressed unless materially changed; a rejected proposal returns only if its distinct-filer count doubles or hard money-flow evidence appears, with the earlier note shown. Kinds: `m02_theme`, `m02_phrase` (accept / reject / leave open), `m02_candidate` (accept = tracked tier), `m02_link` (confirm / flip direction / reject), `m02_input` (confirm / reject); each explained with worked examples in the review guide (D-UI-15 pattern). Order without a combined score: evidence grade, then hard money-flow present, then breadth growth; mention-only proposals sit in a lower "watch" tier (S89). Each proposal shows first-detected date, share vs last year, distinct companies and industries, money flow, 3 sample passages and a **crowding flag** = new fund registrations (N-1A, 485APOS) mentioning the theme phrase per window (ETF launches cluster at peak hype, S90). Reasons: alert volume cuts response (S86); filing text is priced over months, 8-K events in days (S87) (owner).

## Failure modes
| Input or indicator | How it can mislead | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| Phrase counts | Exhibits and boilerplate inflate document counts | Count distinct filings and companies (D-SEC-14) |
| Phrase counts | A theme renamed (e.g. new acronym) looks like a decline | Theme = phrase set; engine proposes synonyms for review |
| Breadth | Many small filers mentioning a buzzword | Breadth by industry and size; money-flow component alongside |
| News | Press-release hype vs substance | Grade A/B shown; filings carry more weight than news |
| LLM phrase/synonym proposals | Plausible but unused phrases; weakest exactly on new themes (S82) | Code mines phrases first; hits + ≥ N companies + industry spread + sample passages before review; zero hits shown as such (D-M02-8) |
| LLM link extraction | Misses most links; direction (who supplies whom) wrong ~1 in 3 when type matters (S85) | Code selects passages; owner confirms each link; precision measured on a labelled set first; missing = unknown (D-M02-9) |
| Capex guidance as money flow | Guidance is a plan; it gets cut or delayed (large structures fall short of plan, S75) | Show as guidance with its period; compare with later XBRL capex and guidance changes (R19) |
| Capex guidance as money flow | Call-only guidance missing → ledger shows stale, lower figures (3 of 4 largest spenders in 2026) | IR documents (D-M02-6); six freshness states per spender and period (D-M02-30, built D-M02-33) |
| Capex guidance as money flow | Definition changes look like cuts (Microsoft finance → operating leases, 2026) | Clause kept per row; "definition changed" label |
| Capex positive for the spender (D-M02-5) | Heavy capex has preceded lower spender returns (S71–S73; weaker since ~2009) | Spender page shows capex growth beside revenue and RPO growth, so the burden is visible next to the positive |
| Customer-capex → supplier link | Not shown by peer-reviewed research (customer returns lead suppliers, S44; capex guidance untested) | Labelled hypothesis until backtested |
| Actual capex on own definition | Extension tags absent from companyfacts; a recipe silently off by a few % | XBRL instance for override/low-coverage companies; stated figure shown when the recipe is approximate or > 1% off; gap review item (D-M02-32) |
| RPO across companies | Contracts ≤ 1 year may be excluded (practical expedient) | Compare each company with its own history only (S79) |
