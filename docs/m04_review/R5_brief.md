## What the research says
- Filings must state causes, including price versus volume (Reg S-K 303(b), S-new-49). Every test case has one: Tesla FY2023 "primarily due to a lower average selling price" (S-new-50); Target's pp waterfall (S-new-51); Invitae's dollar items (S-new-r5-4).
- Falling gross margin predicts lower earnings (S-new-52). Per a secondary source, the construction is % change in sales − % change in gross-profit dollars against the two prior years' mean, annual. The evidence is mixed.
- Sticky costs (S-new-54): a margin falling with revenue can be scale; a margin falling while revenue rises needs a cause, at any stage (Invitae was "introduction" in every year 2019–22).
- Profitability reverts about 38% a year, except in the decline stage (S-new-55, S-new-56).
- Young firms: unit cost should fall with cumulative output (Wright's law, S-new-57).
- Inventory ahead of sales predicts EPS, in an inverted U, for retailers only (S-new-53). The 15 pp gap is unsourced.
- KPI risk: contribution margin is non-GAAP; Rivian's per-vehicle KPI is undefined; credits carry no units (S-new-59).
- Customers are often anonymous (S16). Supplier returns track major customers more when concentration is high and products less unique (S45, full text; concentration as an HHI). RPO matters for contracted names only.
