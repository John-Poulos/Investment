Investment Strategies:
----------------------
Type of investing by time frame:
	1. multi year investments (years)
	2. position trading (months)
	3. swing trading (weeks)

Select the investment area:
	1. Position yourself for where the money and growth is going to be
	2. Fundamental analysis comes first - guides what to invest in.
	3. Technical analysis comes second - guides when to invest in something.

Catch the S-curve early:
	1. Early entry can mean asymmetric payoff
	2. Problem with this approach is latency before appreciation
	3. TSLA post-IPO flat for years, AMD before AI, MU before high bandwith memory
	4. Don't chase after the initial move, position before the crowd notices
	5. Develop a process over "hot" stock tips
	6. The pattern to develop - determine the investment space -> recognize the leader ->
			verify the financials -> determine if they have a competitive moat -> determine entry/exit points

Follow the money:
	1. Estimate how much money will be spent into the future
	2. Identify how it will be spent, how much money has been allocated

Identify who will be the recipient of the the capex
	1. One company may do all the spending and itself maybe a great candidiate
		but what about the companies it is purchasing from - those too maybe
		excellent purchases. Example: The hyperscaler build out being done by Google/Meta etc
		will directly benefit NVDA/AMD for chips, MU, SK Hynix for memory, ALAB, MRVL, AVGO
		for CXL/optics/PCIe connectivity
	2. This has to be done from news and reportings as by the time it is on
		the profit and loss sheet, the information is too old and the stock
		has likely already been evaluated for it.

Identify the leaders in the space:
	1. The top 0.3% of the market make up 1/2 of the market's return over the last century.
	2. Find the leaders by examining their market share, their product design's competitiveness,
		their technology roadmaps and switching costs as parts of their competitive moats.
	3. Is the share accelearting into a growing total addressable market (TAM)?

Some examples of what early entry looks like - AMD, while NVDA stock appreciated much before AMD,
AMD started to gain due to data center gains and the MI300/Instinct roadmap. The software stack
is catching up as well, even though AMD is still discounted versus the NVDA narrative.
AMD has given custom chip signals with segment growth and design wins.

Micron, MU is a memory manufacture. Memory is typically a highly cyclical industry where demand
outsrips supply until it catches up with new capacity. The recent change has been with high bandwith
memory, HBM which has been outstripping supply. There have been nonstop signals from both Elon Musk
and Jensen Huang on the need for HBM based on both their respective companies capex and bill of
materials for memory. This example is one of future demand and their appears to be demand for the
next few years while the data center build outs continue. This is an example of an early signals
- a structural product inside a cyclical ticker.

ALAB and MRVL - both companies create connectivity and custom silicon solutions for AI racks. The
early signal here was revenue CAGR against their peers, gross margin expansion and backlog. Also
their designs have given them competitive edge.

TSLA maybe an example of early based on the TAM that they are positioning themselves for:
self driving cars, self driving taxis, humanoid robots, energy storage and production (solar panels).
These layers can be considered S-curves and depending on implementation and speed of execution could
bring about dramatic changes. TSLA seems well poised because of their integration in these industries.

Analyze the financials:
	1. Review YoY revenue growth and the 3 year CAGR
	2. Look at Free Cash Flow (FCF) growth - it is a golden indicator - example, if a company can continue to
		grow FCF while building out its infrastructure or growing its business in general, like new products,
		etc., it would be considered a very strong signal.
	3. Gross margin trends - if expanding then the company has pricing power in the market
	4. Operating leverage - if their operating expense is growing slower than sales, also a good signal
	5. Consider growth potential of equity: potential growth percent and FCF margin considering TAM's in
		comparison to increases in money supply. Increase in money supply plus inflation creates a percentage
		that you would want to exceed as a return to outpace.
	6. Avoid a company who has increasing revenue while its margins are collapsing usually indicates a strong
		competitor. For exmaple - Invitae

Financial Red Flags:
	1. Look for cash burn with no share gains
	2. Serial stock dilution for the company to survive
	3. Accounting manipulations and "one-time" adjustments. - examples GE, Rivian - multiple one time adjustments
	4. Massive negative FCF
	5. Revenue up but unit economics down
	6. Debt - large debt against revenue - avoid

CAPEX and Future Spend:
	1. If capital expenditure (CAPEX) is done to create an economic and competitive advantage (moat) - the spend
		can be viewed as a beneficial or positive flag. The determination is subjective and would need expert
		opinions to be weighed, but if a company is positioning itself to be the lead in a field by spending
		money to secure its future and if they have a proven track record of success, this is a positive flag.
	2. CAPEX and sales rising into a demand wave can be bullsih.
		examples: MU HBM fabs buildout, AMD struggling with increasing capacity, TSLA giga/lithium factories
	3. Separate growth capex from maintenance capex and pair it with return on invested capital to create a signal
	4. CAPEX should be viewed in context with return on invested capital (ROIC) and customer prepayments or
		LTA's - long term agreements.
	5. CAPEX without demand destroys value, follow the bookings.

Moat Check:
	1. Don't invest in a company without a moat.
	2. Vertical integration - exmaples TSLA - scale, NVDA - CUDA lock in ecosystem
	3. Process node or supply or IP (intellectual property)
		- examples MU has HBM supply that others do not, ALAB products, supply control of transformers for data
		center buildouts, etc
	4. Custom ASIC relationships - AMD/AVGO working with Google. MRVL and its customers - one factor is switching cost
	5. Timeframe - can a competitor catch up in 3 years or less? If not, the company has a moat.

Ownership and Insiders:
	1. Discern between instiutonal quality not just percent ownership - the difference between smart and dumb money.
	2. Look at 13F filings with buys from the smart money as a soft signal - CEO of equity, Stanley Druckenmiller, etc.
	3. Insider buying should exceed selling with context given to scheduled 10b5-1 sales which can sometimes be noise.
	4. Look at how much short interest there is as it can be fuel for squeezes or structural risk.
		-example of insider activity of note - CRWV - insiders sold 25.2M shares in last 9-12 months worth >$3.4B

Analysts Price Targets and Upgrades:
	1. Use as a sentiment guide as half or more of analysts are wrong.
	2. Look at spread of targets and revision direction
	3. Estimate revisions for EPS and revenue over the next 2 years
	4. Wallstreet always misses new S-curve opportunities
	5. Crowded consensus means less asymmetry - edge fades when everyone knows about the deal.

PEG:
	1. Useful for finding S-curve companies	as those companies PE (price to earnings) ration may be very high.
		But when taken in context with the expected annial earning per share growth - PE/EPS growth rate = PEG
		it makes it easier to spot a company that is going to grow.
	2. Ideal PEG ration is 1, anything below that is a great opportunity.

Technical Analysis:
	1. Don't buy a stock because its cheap or completly oversold - 80% of the time it's dead or dying
	2. Use augmented trading range or create one to give guidance on where a good entry point exists
	3. Create a tool for mean reversion to signify top and bottom extremes. This can be based off of 1-2 standard
		deviations from the running average of the stock price. Need to determine what range for that average is
		ideal. This will aid in selling calls at tops and selling puts at bottoms.
	4. Buy signals need to be based on a confluence of multiple signals and flags, not just one.

Understand why an asset is or is not moving:
	1. Understand the macro - wars, interest rates, politics, the narrative, future earnings, money flows, short squeezes
	2. Be wary of late capital chasing the same story
	3. If you dont know why something is moving, you dont have an edge
	4. Be able to write a sentence - this asset is moving because ....
	5. It is acceptable to have the knowledge of an edge and the asset is not moving, may indicate that you are
		early and the market has not priced in yet. example: Solana trades at 1/5 of the market cap of Etherium but
		does 5000% more activity.

Entry, Size, Exit and Killswitch:
	1. Know exit before entry and write it down as part of the plan
	2. Optimize position size - balance risk and reward
	3. Know the killswitch: and event that changes the investment idea - ban on AI, capex slowdown, etc.
	4. Opportunity cost vs. core safety. Capital is finite so above all else, perserve capital - do not lose.
	5. Review the thesis quarterly - fill or kill
